// Studio · Process · Press

Behind the cache.

Twelve people in two cities. One commit per season. We don't run sales, we don't restock, and we don't sell to the resale market — this is how that works in practice.

// 01 — Studio

Two cities, one commit.

.cache started as a private GitHub org in 2024 — two people, three friends, a shared dotfiles repo. Now it's twelve, split between a converted print shop in Kreuzberg and a tatami-floor studio in Nakameguro. Everything else came after.

We make clothes for people who ship software. That's not a marketing line — it's the literal job. Half the studio writes code in the morning and pattern-cuts in the afternoon. The other half does the reverse.

Every drop starts as a thread in a private channel. Someone says: "we should make a tee that's actually opaque." Or: "the hoodie I want for cold rooms doesn't exist." The thread gets a tag. If it survives a season of arguing, it gets a pattern. If the pattern survives a mill visit, it gets a run number.

"Once it ships, the run closes. There is no v1.1 of a t-shirt."

We cap runs at 500 pieces because that's what twelve people can ship — sign, inspect, fold, package — in three weekends without compromising. We tried 800 once. We don't anymore.

The brand name is a joke our infra engineer made in 2023 — a cache is a small, fast layer between you and the thing you actually need. We liked that as a description of a wardrobe. We kept it.

// 02 — Process

Commit, cut, ship.

Five steps from thread to shipped piece. Average cycle is eleven weeks. The slowest part is the mill, the fastest is packing — we wish it was the other way around.

01
Week 1 — 2 · Studio thread

Open the issue.

Someone proposes a piece in our private channel. It gets a tag, a one-line spec, and an opinion vote from the twelve. If it survives the week, it gets a draft pattern.

ToolingLinear · Notion
Approve9 of 12 needed
OutputSpec doc · sketch
02
Week 3 — 5 · Sample

Cut three samples.

We block-print and machine-sew three samples in the Berlin studio. Members in Tokyo wear them for two weeks. If something rips, we fix it. If nothing rips, we still fix something.

PatternsHand · Clo3D verify
Wear test14 days · 6 wearers
Yield3 → 1 rev cycle
03
Week 6 — 9 · Mill

Send to mill.

We work with six small mills — three in Portugal, two in Japan, one in northern Italy. Each gets the spec and a sample. We visit before production starts. We don't approve the run remotely.

Mills06 · PT / JP / IT
VisitPre-prod required
Run500 max
04
Week 10 · Sign + inspect

Hand-sign, hand-inspect.

The whole studio shows up. Every piece gets pulled, inspected, signed inside the back hem. We catch ~3% defects this way. They go to the studio archive — wear-test stock for the next cycle.

HandsAll 12
Defects~3% caught
SignedInside back hem
05
Week 11 · Ship + archive

Tag and ship.

Members get 96 hours first. Then it opens to everyone until it's gone. When the last piece ships, we cut a tag — git tag v1.0 — and the run closes. No restock, no apology. The archive page goes live.

Five fabrics. That's it.

We picked five and we don't add. Every piece across every drop comes from this stack. That's how a tee from Drop 001 will still look like a sibling of a hoodie from Drop 004 — same yarn family, same wash, same hand.
